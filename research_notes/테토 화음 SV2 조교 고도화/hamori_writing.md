# ハモリ (vocal harmony) writing for J-pop / Vocaloid: composition and arrangement rules

Scope: how to write the notes, not how to mix them. Source reliability tags used below:
- [PRACT-SCHOOL]: a commercial vocal school or DTM site column. Practitioner consensus, not academic.
- [PRACT-BLOG]: a single practitioner blog or note.com post.
- [MAG]: an established trade magazine (Sound On Sound).
- [GENERAL]: Wikipedia or a general lesson site.
No academic or official (Berklee-published) source with explicit pop-harmony rules was reachable in this session. See Gaps.

## 1. 上ハモ vs 下ハモ, intervals, and chord clashes

### Takeaway
Japanese practitioners agree on these defaults. Use diatonic parallel 3rds, with 6ths as the softer or octave-shifted alternative. Use 4ths or 5ths only when a 3rd or 6th lands on an avoid note or tension. Never sustain a 2nd or 7th against the lead. Default to 下ハモ (supports the lead; verses, quiet parts, "when in doubt"). Use 上ハモ for サビ and climaxes, kept quieter so it doesn't 食う (swallow) the lead. Choose by chord fit and the singer's range, and switching sides mid-song is allowed. On every sustained or strong-beat note, check the harmony note against the chord, not just the scale.

### Cited Findings
**Intervals**
- Use 3rds and 6ths as the base, and 4ths and 5ths at key points ("基本は3度、6度のインターバルの音を使用し、要所要所で4度、5度のインターバルの音を使用する"). Reason: 3rds and 6ths blend best, while 4ths and 5ths "blend too much" (音が馴染みすぎる) [PRACT-BLOG] — [ongakuriron.com](https://ongakuriron.com/hamori)
- 3度上 means "明るさ・華やかさ・心地よさ". 3度下 means "落ち着き・太さ・重厚感". Avoid the 2度 and 7度 against the lead because they sound muddy (濁りやすい) [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [NOPPO MUSIC SCHOOL (vocaloid course blog)](https://noppo-music.co.jp/course/voice-training/vocaloid/blog/12002/)
- The 3rd is the most common and most natural interval. Use the 6th when the 3rd is hard to sing (octave-shift the 3rd). The 4th is rare: use it when the melody is a tension a 3rd won't fit. Octave unison reinforces the melody but "isn't really harmony". Generally avoid the 5th as a parallel line unless intended, because it sounds floaty (浮遊感) and lacks harmonic depth [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- The 5th is "wide and powerful" (サビ盛り上げ, 掛け声). Octave adds thickness (男女デュエット, end of サビ) [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [starroute.jp](https://www.starroute.jp/column/harmony-tsukurikata/)
- The 6th is softer and wider than the 3rd and adds thickness while keeping the lead in front. The 5th suits rock and folk. The 2nd is too close and muddy, so avoid it unless intended [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- A constant interval is usually a 3rd above or a 6th below. 4ths and 5ths above add little because they already occur as overtones. Occasionally insert a 4th among 3rds, or a 4th or 6th among 5ths [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art 作曲図書室](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)
- 3rds and 6ths are complements (inversions), and so are 4ths and 5ths. If the melody is the chord's 5th, a 3rd above adds a 7th-type color that may not fit. If the melody is the chord's 3rd, a 6th above lands on the root. "Try sixths as readily as thirds" [GENERAL, English lesson] — [Signals Music Studio](https://signalsmusic.studio/lessons/writing-harmonies)
- The simplest pop backup is a single line above or below the main vocal, often in 3rds or 6ths [GENERAL] — [Wikipedia: Vocal harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_harmony)

**上ハモ / 下ハモ choice**
- 下ハモ: calm, thick, stable, and easy for anyone to enter. It suits Aメロ and quiet parts but can get buried. 上ハモ: bright and showy, suits サビ and climaxes, but risks 食う (overpowering) the lead. "迷ったら下ハモから". Pattern example: "サビの前半は下ハモで支えて、いちばん盛り上がる最後のフレーズだけ上ハモ" [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [starroute.jp](https://www.starroute.jp/column/harmony-tsukurikata/)
- Basic combination: "サビは上ハモ、Aメロやブリッジは下ハモ". If the 上ハモ exceeds the singer's range, choose 下ハモ. Keep 上ハモ quieter because high notes catch the ear [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Either direction works, and you can switch mid-song. **Chord fit decides**. Upper stands out, so subdue it (e.g. falsetto). Lower supports and stays less prominent [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- 3度下 is "the most basic style" and the first to try ("とりあえずハモりたい…3度下"). The lower voice supports, so the lead stays dominant [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [TuneGate.me](https://tunegate.me/P20151021001)
- Switching between above and below within a passage is allowed, as is mixing 3度 and 6度 in one line [GENERAL] — [riron.sitefactory.info](https://riron.sitefactory.info/0601.html)

**Clash handling (avoid notes and tensions)**
- An avoid note is a scale tone a half step above a chord tone. Examples (key C): F over Cmaj7, F over Am7, C and F over Em, G over Dmaj7 (key D). They are not absolutely banned: they're fine if short and not emphasized ("長く伸ばしたり、目立つ音として使用しなければ大丈夫"), and weak or off-beats are less affected (弱拍や裏拍上のアボイド・ノートは影響が出にくい) [PRACT-SCHOOL, major DTM school] — [Sleepfreaks: Avoid Notes](https://sleepfreaks-dtm.com/en/music-theory-beginner/avoid-note)
- Mistake 1: always using a 3rd. Over CMaj9 with a sustained melody D, the 3rd above is F (avoid note), so change it to G. Mistake 2: over Gsus4 with a sustained melody G, the 3rd above is B, which clashes with the chord's C, so change it to C. Rule: "基本的には3度でハモるが、コードに合わない音（かつ長めの音）のときは、4度などに変更する" [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- On passing or non-emphasized notes, keep the 3rd or 6th as is ("経過音などの強調されない音の場合はそのまま3度、6度を使用して大丈夫"). When a 3rd or 6th is dissonant against the chord, switch to a 4th or 5th. The author occasionally uses 減5度 / 増4度 for color [PRACT-BLOG] — [ongakuriron.com](https://ongakuriron.com/hamori)
- Worked case: melody C over a C chord. The 3rd below (A) is not a chord tone. Options: (1) move to E (6th below, a chord tone), (2) keep A (acceptable), (3) use the 6th. Don't repeat option 2 for many consecutive notes or the result becomes unclear (濁る). If one held harmony note spans two chords, split it into two notes [GENERAL] — [riron.sitefactory.info](https://riron.sitefactory.info/0601.html)
- The chord can override the scale. Melody B over E7 (resolving to Am) gets a harmony of G♯ (major 3rd below), not the diatonic G, because the dominant E7→Am requires it. This matters for minor keys (harmonic-minor V) [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [TuneGate.me](https://tunegate.me/P20151021001)
- When parallel 3rds or 6ths don't fit, you can (a) keep parallel motion and let it imply tensions (which may shift the original feel), or (b) keep the chord and use other chord tones such as a 4th or 5th, which preserves the original image [PRACT-BLOG, DTM musician KFS] — [note.com/kfs_kfs](https://note.com/kfs_kfs/n/nbc6b6a162039)
- "ハモリは無理をしないことが重要。協和を重視". Don't force a 3rd onto a tension note. A harmony landing on the 6th or 9th of the chord is acceptable. Avoid "アヴォイドの響き" (e.g. F over C). A deliberate 2nd clash can be used, either resolving to a 3rd or left as is [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)
- Pick a chord tone: in a C chord with the melody on ミ, the harmony is ソ or ド. Procedure: write out the melody, check the chord, pick the 3rd from the scale, confirm it's a chord tone, change it if it sounds wrong [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Pop example: with a C major chord and the lead on C, the harmony voices should ideally take E and G [PRACT-BLOG, vendor blog] — [Kits.AI](https://www.kits.ai/blog/vocal-harmony-melody-music-genre)

### Inferences
- These rules imply a per-note algorithm: (1) compute the diatonic 3rd (above or below per section policy). (2) If the note is "important" (long, on a strong beat, or a phrase end), check it against the current chord. If it's a chord tone or allowed tension (6/9), keep it. If it's an avoid note (a half step above a chord tone), a sus clash, or forms a 2nd or 7th with the lead, try the 6th (same side), then the nearest chord tone a 4th or 5th away. (3) If the note is "unimportant" (short, weak beat, passing), keep the parallel 3rd even if it's non-chord. (4) In minor keys, raise the 3rd or 7th degree over V (E7-type) chords. In F minor, for example, use E♮ not E♭ over C7.
- Interval preferences for a "safe" output, in order: 3rd, then 6th, then 4th, then 5th (singly, never consecutively). Never sustain a 2nd or 7th, and never land in unison with the lead.

### Gaps
- No source gave numeric thresholds for "long note" (e.g. ≥ 1 beat). Practitioners say only 長めの音 / 伸ばす音. A threshold of about ≥ 1 beat at 120 to 150 BPM, or any phrase-final note, is my inference.
- No source gave register-based rules (e.g. "if the lead is above C5, use 下ハモ") beyond "if 上ハモ exceeds the range, use 下ハモ".

## 2. Rhythm and simplification (音数, sustaining, melismas)

### Takeaway
There are two distinct practices. (a) Lyrical ハモリ (歌詞付き) moves in the **same rhythm and the same lyrics** as the lead, with consonant onsets and cutoffs (切る位置) aligned exactly. (b) コーラス / backing (ウー・アー, 白玉) is accompaniment and uses rougher, simpler rhythm: hold long chord tones per chord, with minimal motion. Explicit "reduce note count" rules for lyrical harmony are rare in sources. The supported versions are these: keep the harmony line flatter and less mobile than the melody, add harmony only to phrase-end sustained notes, and don't copy runs.

### Cited Findings
- Lyrical ハモリ uses the "same rhythm, same lyrics, only pitch shifted" principle. Align the consonant onsets and breath positions with the lead [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [starroute.jp](https://www.starroute.jp/column/harmony-tsukurikata/); same rule ("歌メロと同じリズムと歌詞で、3度下の音程を歌う") — [TuneGate.me](https://tunegate.me/P20151021001)
- Match pronunciation timing and cutoff positions. Example: if the harmony stretches "らあ", it sounds misaligned with the lead's "ら". Sing harmony "抑揚を抑え、まっすぐ" (flatter dynamics, straight tone) [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Harmony with lyrics follows the melody's rhythm. コーラス is accompaniment-side with rougher rhythms than the melody. The simplest form holds long notes per chord, voices move little, and the 3rd may drop out. Make the harmony line **flatter and less mobile than the melody** [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)
- 単純白玉コードコーラス: hold chord tones in whole notes, mostly ignoring the melody, for lift in サビ. Use about 3 voices. When thinning dense chords, drop the 5th first, then the root (the bass covers it), then far tensions. For C9, keep the 3rd, 7th, and 9th. Use inversions so each voice moves by step (e.g. Dm7–G7–C7, with voices moving by a semitone or whole tone) [PRACT-BLOG] — [note.com/kfs_kfs](https://note.com/kfs_kfs/n/nbc6b6a162039)
- With independent rhythm, the harmony can fill gaps or respond while the lead holds a note. Dense independent polyphony is rare in pop because the lyrics become hard to hear [PRACT-BLOG] — [note.com/kfs_kfs](https://note.com/kfs_kfs/n/nbc6b6a162039)
- One placement option is to harmonize only the sustained note at the end of a phrase (フレーズの終わりの伸ばす音だけ) [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Some bands use long, slow-moving harmony notes supporting the lead during choruses. Backup rhythm must be in time with the lead and the rhythm section [GENERAL] — [Wikipedia: Vocal harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_harmony)
- Two or three voices that follow the lead's phrasing exactly often sound more powerful than dense stacks [PRACT-BLOG, vendor] — [Kits.AI](https://www.kits.ai/blog/vocal-harmony-melody-music-genre)
- Harmony with mismatched rhythm "バラバラに聴こえてしまいます" [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [UtaTen](https://utaten.com/karaoke/harmonizing/)
- Japanese practitioner on Vocaloid harmony: keep the harmony's pitch curve smooth (赤い線があんまりバラバラにならないように) because a jagged harmony pitch line sounds unnatural [PRACT-BLOG] — [scrapbox musicstudyroom](https://scrapbox.io/musicstudyroom/%E3%83%9C%E3%82%AB%E3%83%AD%E3%81%AE%E5%A3%B0%E3%81%AE%E3%83%8F%E3%83%A2%E3%83%AA%E6%96%B9)

### Inferences
- For a program, this suggests the following. In lyrical mode, mirror the lead's onsets and offsets exactly, but merge melismatic or fast ornamental notes (several pitches on one syllable) into a single sustained chord-tone note for that syllable. This follows from "flatter and less mobile" plus "don't force". Optionally drop the harmony on fast runs (e.g. 16ths at 150 BPM) and resume on the next held or strong-beat note. Do not add vibrato or しゃくり at lead-level depth ("抑揚を抑え、まっすぐ").
- In pad mode (ウー/アー), generate whole-note or half-note chord tones per chord change with stepwise voice leading. These are independent of the lead's syllables.

### Gaps
- No source states a numeric reduction ratio (e.g. "harmony has X% of the lead's notes"). The explicit claim "ハモリはメロより音数を減らす" could not be found in a citable source. It may be oral practitioner wisdom.
- No source specifically addressed how to treat melismas in lyrical harmony. The Wikipedia/Kits.AI search summary's melisma advice was the search tool's own suggestion, not sourced, and is excluded.

## 3. Where to put harmony in a song

### Takeaway
There is a consistent practitioner consensus on staging. Leave Aメロ bare (lyrics need clarity). Optionally harmonize the second half of Bメロ to build lift. Harmony belongs mainly in サビ: the whole サビ, or just the hook or key words. Escalate across the song: 1番 下ハモ only, 2番 add 上ハモ, ラスサビ both (three voices). Leaving sections bare ("入れない勇気") is what makes the additions audible.

### Cited Findings
- Aメロ: lead only ("歌詞を聴かせる場面…シンプルなほうが伝わります"). Bメロ: harmonize only the second half to build toward サビ. サビ: the best place, either all of it or just the memorable phrases. Escalation example: 1番 下ハモ only, 2番 add 上ハモ, ラスサビ both. Adding a 2nd voice where there was 1 "明確に盛り上がります". Full-song harmony habituates the listener, so "入れない勇気も必要". Varying harmony per scene avoids blur [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Don't necessarily harmonize the whole サビ. Add it at key phrases, hooks, or important words, and leave other parts bare on purpose [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- Use harmony in some blocks and not others, or on a single phrase, for contrast. Traditionally, harmony goes on lyric phrases implying togetherness [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)
- Beginners should start with just 4 bars of サビ. You don't need the same harmony throughout the song [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [starroute.jp](https://www.starroute.jp/column/harmony-tsukurikata/)
- Use harmony where you want emphasis (サビ, climax): "特に聴かせたい部分で厚みを出す" [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [NOPPO](https://noppo-music.co.jp/course/voice-training/vocaloid/blog/12002/)
- Harmonies "don't usually appear until the melody has been firmly established". Single harmony lines suit verses (recorded once or twice at most). Save massed multi-tracked BVs for the song's high point. If there's a three-part harmony on line 5 of verse 1, you'll likely need something similar in the same spot later (consistency across repeats). Octave doubles work best on whole sections [MAG] — [Sound On Sound: Crafting Perfect Pop Backing Vocals](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/crafting-perfect-pop-backing-vocals)
- "やたらに入れれば良いというものでもなく" (it's not good to add them indiscriminately). Main vocal first, then design harmony [PRACT, label Q&A] — [Sony Music HIT&RUN Q&A](https://www.sonymusic.co.jp/Music/Info/HITandRUN/miya/q115.html)
- ウー/アー chorus (wordless) is recommended for ballads, R&B, and soul. Chord-type harmony (hold chord tones, common tones across changes) uses three voices for clear chord color and four or more for tension chords [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)

### Inferences
- These sources suggest a default plan for a Vocaloid song (F minor 150 BPM or A♭ major 126 BPM). 1A and 1B: none (or a 2nd-half-of-B 下ハモ). 1サビ: 下ハモ on the hook lines plus phrase-end sustains. 2サビ: add 上ハモ on the climactic last line. Bridge or 落ちサビ: optional ウー pad or none. ラスサビ: 上ハモ plus 下ハモ (three voices), optionally with a ウー/アー 白玉 pad. Keep placement consistent between repeated sections (Sound On Sound).

### Gaps
- No source with Vocaloid-specific statistics (e.g. how many popular ボカロ songs harmonize the first サビ) was found.
- Call-and-response (掛け合い) backing was not covered in any fetched source beyond KFS's "fill the gaps when the lead holds".

## 4. Voice leading, crossing, ranges, spacing

### Takeaway
Pop practitioners relax classical rules but keep the core ones. Avoid **consecutive** parallel 5ths and octaves against the lead (an isolated 5th is fine). Avoid unisons with the lead because they cause a level jump. Prefer stepwise harmony lines. A harmony crossing through the lead is acceptable if it never lands on a unison. When both 上ハモ and 下ハモ use 3rds, they sit a 5th apart, which sounds floaty and is acceptable in studio. No numeric female-range limits were found in sources.

### Cited Findings
- Consecutive perfect 5ths against the melody (2 or more in a row) are 禁則 in theory. Non-consecutive 5ths are fine. Consecutive octaves are also 禁則, but rock uses both, and they can sound good (e.g. 布袋寅泰 "RUSSIAN ROULETTE" solo moves octave → 3rd → 5th harmony) [PRACT-BLOG] — [ongakuriron.com](https://ongakuriron.com/hamori)
- Consecutive 5ths sound stiff, so change them to a 3rd or 6th. 並達五度 (hidden 5ths) are fine in pop. Contrapuntal harmony: keep the line mostly stepwise and leap only on the same beat as the lead. On beats 1 and 3, use a chord tone a 3rd or 6th from the melody, or a 4th or 5th if impossible. Prefer contrary or oblique motion, and limit parallel motion to about 3 steps in a row. After a diminished 5th (e.g. B–F), the next interval must be a 3rd or 6th [PRACT-BLOG] — [note.com/kfs_kfs](https://note.com/kfs_kfs/n/nbc6b6a162039)
- With 上ハモ and 下ハモ both at a 3rd, the harmonies are a 5th apart and can sound floaty. Studio work tolerates this, but live performance should follow the rules strictly [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- In three-part harmony, the outer lines are a 5th apart. Avoid the tritone between them unless intended [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)
- The classical baseline is no parallel octaves, fifths, or unisons. Adjacent upper voices should stay within an octave. Avoid melodic augmented 2nds and 4ths. Don't double tendency tones (the leading tone, the chordal 7th) [ACADEMIC, part-writing handouts] — [Univ. of Vermont part-writing](https://www.uvm.edu/~dfeurzei/110/materials/partwriting.pdf); [Puget Sound Music Theory: Avoiding Objectionable Parallels](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/AvoidingObjectionableParallels.html) (via search summaries; not fetched in full)
- One pop lesson says the parallel-5ths restriction "doesn't apply to modern rock" [GENERAL] — [Signals Music Studio](https://signalsmusic.studio/lessons/writing-harmonies). This contradicts ongakuriron and KFS, who still advise against **consecutive** 5ths for sung harmony.
- Lines may pass through the melody to link sections and reduce "blockiness" (usually crossing from above to below). Ensure "no unison notes are created", because a unison doubles amplitude and causes a level jump. If a harmony sits above the singer's comfortable range, replace it with a synth or guitar [MAG] — [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/crafting-perfect-pop-backing-vocals)
- Avoid abruptly inserted non-chord tones at hard spots. Don't make lines too fancy at the singer's expense [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)

### Inferences
- These rules translate to algorithm constraints. Reject any harmony note that forms a unison or octave with the lead on consecutive notes, or a perfect 5th on 2 or more consecutive notes. Allow a single 5th or 4th as a substitute. Prefer the candidate with the smallest melodic leap from the previous harmony note (stepwise). Distance from the lead is typically a 3rd (3 to 4 semitones) or 6th (8 to 9 semitones). Anything wider than an octave isn't described as ハモリ in any source.
- For female voice (Kasane Teto SV2), no source gave limits. My uncited guess is to keep 上ハモ at or below the lead's own top note in that song, or about A5 at most. When the lead already sits high (e.g. ≥ D5 in an F minor サビ), take 下ハモ, consistent with "上ハモが音域を超えるなら下ハモ". Treat this as a heuristic to verify by ear.

### Gaps
- No sourced numeric vocal-range limits for female harmony parts. No academic pop-harmony source (Berklee) was successfully fetched. The Deke Sharon 10-step manual PDF returned 404.

## 5. Common mistakes (変なハモリ) and diagnosis

### Takeaway
Most "weird" harmony comes from four causes. (1) A mechanical fixed-3rd copy that lands on an avoid or sus-clash note during a sustained note. (2) Too much harmony, or 上ハモ too loud or prominent, which buries the lead. (3) Misaligned onsets, cutoffs, or breaths. (4) Wrong accidentals when copy-shifting (out-of-key notes, or missing the raised 3rd over V in minor). Pros diagnose by checking each long or strong-beat note against the chord on piano and by soloing the harmony with the lead.

### Cited Findings
- Fixed-3rd errors: CMaj9 with melody D gives F (avoid), fix to G. Gsus4 with melody G gives B (clashes with C), fix to C [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- If the lead is hard to hear: reduce the number of harmonies, change to 下ハモ, or change the 3rd to a 6th. If it's muddy: reselect from chord tones. If it stands out: lower the volume and soften the delivery [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Listed failures: adding harmony before the lead is stable, using the same interval everywhere, harmony too loud, misaligned breaths, harmonizing the whole song, choosing notes "なんとなく". Guide level is harmony 60 to 80 if the lead is 100 [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [starroute.jp](https://www.starroute.jp/column/harmony-tsukurikata/) (independent-artist suggests 1/3 to 1/2 of the lead's volume as a starting point. The two disagree, and this is mixing territory anyway)
- Copy-and-shift DTM method: copy the lead, move it 3 scale steps up or down, then fix notes that fall outside the key one by one (e.g. in G major only F is sharp) [PRACT-BLOG, note.com] — [note.com/tebachan](https://note.com/tebachan/n/n50c347a36ea7)
- Forcing 3rds onto tensions causes pitch errors. Writing avoid notes and making lines too fancy are also listed. Japanese bands often accept slightly off harmony, while overseas practice is less bound to 3rds [PRACT-BLOG] — [musicsounds.art](https://musicsounds.art/harmony_chorus/)
- Unison collisions with the melody (level jumps), confusing lyric collisions when the harmony words differ, and too many layers so that individual voices can't be picked out [MAG] — [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/crafting-perfect-pop-backing-vocals)
- Check candidate notes on piano or guitar before tracking. "usually another note works as well or better" [MAG] — [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/crafting-perfect-pop-backing-vocals)
- Keeping a clashing (non-chord) 3rd is OK once, but repeating it over many notes becomes unclear (濁る) [GENERAL] — [riron.sitefactory.info](https://riron.sitefactory.info/0601.html)

### Inferences
- Automated "変なハモリ" checks could flag the following: sustained (long) harmony notes that are a half step above a chord tone; 2nds or 7ths with the lead on strong beats; unisons with the lead; 2 or more consecutive P5 or P8; a harmony louder than or equal to the lead; harmony onsets or offsets differing from the lead's in lyrical mode; and harmony density, such as harmony present in more than one section type before the final サビ.

### Gaps
- No pro-producer (ボカロP) interview describing their own diagnostic process was found.

## 6. Step-by-step guides found (summary of procedures)

### Takeaway
Japanese guides converge on one procedure. Fix the lead and chords first, copy the lead, shift it diatonically by a 3rd (下 by default), check each note against the chord, change clashing long or strong notes to a 6th, a chord tone, or a 4th or 5th, keep short passing notes parallel, limit placement to key phrases, then audition with the lead.

### Cited Findings
- 5-step (vocal-school): perfect the lead, pick one spot (e.g. the start of サビ), find a 3度下 that blends, practice at half tempo alternating the lead and the harmony, then overlay at full tempo [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [starroute.jp](https://www.starroute.jp/column/harmony-tsukurikata/)
- Pick-by-chord procedure: write the melody, check the chord, take the 3rd from the scale, confirm it's a chord tone, change it if it's off, then play it and listen [PRACT-BLOG] — [independent-artist.net](https://independent-artist.net/4204/)
- Chord-tone procedure: check the bar's chord, list its tones (C = C-E-G, Am = A-C-E), and choose a chord tone that doesn't clash with the melody note [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [NOPPO](https://noppo-music.co.jp/course/voice-training/vocaloid/blog/12002/)
- Five harmony types by increasing difficulty (KFS): 白玉 chord chorus, then a leap-minimized chord chorus, then parallel 3rds or 6ths, then same-rhythm counterpoint, then independent-rhythm counterpoint [PRACT-BLOG] — [note.com/kfs_kfs](https://note.com/kfs_kfs/n/nbc6b6a162039)
- Harmony types (nomadial): parallel (3rd, 6th, 4th, octave, 5th), chord-based (held chord tones, common tones), and ウーアーコーラス [PRACT-BLOG] — [nomadial.com](https://nomadial.com/chorus/)
- Ear-training method: sing chord tones in the order 1→5→3 on "uh" for each chord C through B. Harmony must match rhythm as well as pitch [PRACT-SCHOOL] — [UtaTen](https://utaten.com/karaoke/harmonizing/)

### Inferences
- Combining these into a program pipeline gives the following steps.
  1. Segment the song into sections and choose a harmony policy per section (none, 下, 上, both, pad).
  2. For each lead note in a harmonized line, generate candidates: diatonic 3rd (same side), 6th, chord tones within a 6th, then 4th or 5th.
  3. Score candidates. Chord-tone or allowed-tension membership is weighted by note importance (duration, strong beat, phrase end). Apply penalties for avoid notes, 2nds or 7ths with the lead, unisons, consecutive P5 or P8, leaps larger than a 4th in the harmony, and out-of-range notes. Add a bonus for keeping the previous interval type (parallel continuity) and for stepwise motion.
  4. Simplify: merge melisma or fast-run notes into held notes, and optionally drop harmony on runs.
  5. Copy the lead's lyric and onsets in lyrical mode, or use "あ"/"う" whole or half notes per chord in pad mode.
  6. Voice it below the lead in level, with a straighter tone (less vibrato and pitch deviation).
- Note that the per-section policy and the scoring weights are synthesized from practitioner consensus, not from any single authoritative rulebook.
